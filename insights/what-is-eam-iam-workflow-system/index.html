What Are EAMs and IAMs—and What Workflow Systems Do They Need?

EAM and IAM teams may be lean, yet they coordinate complex work across clients, banks, accounts, products and reporting obligations.

See the implementation frameworkOriginal WeChat article
31 Aug 2026Website editionTuroid

Short answer

EAMs and IAMs need a lightweight, reviewable operating layer that connects existing banks and files without recreating a heavyweight private-bank core.

Workflow design

Start with the operating process, not a feature list

A useful system connects data, roles, judgement, review and delivery. These are the three capabilities to establish first.

01

Cross-bank client view

See clients, accounts, holdings and open work while preserving existing custody relationships.

02

Lean-team coordination

Give relationship, investment and operations teams a shared hand-off and review context.

03

Explainable delivery

Connect reports, product follow-ups and client communication to source data and owners.

Implementation path

Begin with one high-friction workflow

Establish the smallest credible workflow, then expand from real use, exceptions and team feedback.

Recommended sequence

  1. Unify client and account master data
  2. Connect bank files and product documents
  3. Create a daily exception queue
  4. Assign role-based access and actions
  5. Review before external delivery
  6. Expand by client and workflow
