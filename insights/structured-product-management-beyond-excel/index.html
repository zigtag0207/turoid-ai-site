Why Structured Product Management Needs More Than Excel

Excel can record product data, but observation dates, coupons, barriers, autocalls and client follow-ups require an active lifecycle workflow.

See the implementation frameworkOriginal WeChat article
31 Aug 2026Website editionTuroid

Short answer

The problem is not that Excel has no value. Structured products are not static holdings: teams need event awareness, ownership and follow-up history.

Workflow design

Start with the operating process, not a feature list

A useful system connects data, roles, judgement, review and delivery. These are the three capabilities to establish first.

01

Products keep generating events

Observation, coupon, barrier, redemption and maturity events change the next action.

02

Risk needs early visibility

Teams should see products approaching barriers, maturity or client-contact points.

03

Follow-up needs context

Decisions, files, client communication and ownership should remain attached to the product.

Implementation path

Begin with one high-friction workflow

Establish the smallest credible workflow, then expand from real use, exceptions and team feedback.

Recommended sequence

  1. Import and validate product master data
  2. Create event calendars and trigger conditions
  3. Identify upcoming material events daily
  4. Assign follow-up ownership
  5. Record decisions and client communication
  6. Preserve lifecycle history after settlement
